A · Transcript tab

Recommended

The player page stays as it is. The row under the controls becomes Chapters · Transcript · History. Transcript follows playback line by line, the line being said is filled, and the word being said is marked.

How it works

  • Tap a line to jump there. Long press or select text to highlight, same as articles.
  • Search icon in the tab filters and jumps to matches.
  • One status line says what's ready: "Ready to 2:41:00 · Mac is transcribing Ch 6".
  • Chapters not done yet show a card with Transcribe now.

Trade-off

  • Cover and controls take the top half, so you see 6–8 lines unless you scroll up past them.

B · Full-screen read-along

Most immersive

A Transcript button (subtitles) in the top bar flips the page into big, lyrics-style text on a dark background. The current sentence is large and bright, the next ones fade. Controls shrink into a dock at the bottom.

How it works

  • Tap a sentence to jump. Scroll freely; a Back to now chip brings it back.
  • Search and Highlight in the ⋮ menu.
  • The screen stays on while it is open.

Trade-off

  • Separate mode to enter and leave; less context (time stamps, chapter list hidden).

C · Read along in the ebook

Extra, on top of A or B

When the same book is in your Ebooks, the transcript is matched to the ebook text, so you read the real book pages (proper paragraphs, spelling, italics) with the spoken sentence highlighted. Whisper's words are only used to line the two up.

How it works

  • A chip on the player: Read along: Ebook · Transcript. Picked automatically when titles match; you can link a book by hand.
  • Your ebook position follows the audio, so switching from listening to reading lands on the same sentence (and back).
  • Highlights land in the ebook, so they show up in both.

Trade-off

  • Needs the ebook. Abridged audiobooks or intros that differ from the text fall back to the plain transcript for those parts.

CHAPTER FIVE · THE FROST

The first frost came early that year, a week before anyone had picked the late apples. In the village they said it was the river's fault, or the moon's, depending on who you asked. Ada walked the rows at dawn, counting the trees that had already lost their leaves. Her father had always said the orchard kept its own calendar. She had never believed him until now.

At the far wall the old pear tree was still green, as if it had not heard the news. She put her hand on the bark. It was warm.
